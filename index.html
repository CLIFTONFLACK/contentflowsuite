01

Sound familiar?

Most WordPress sites run on a theme, an AI writer, an automation tool and a feedback tool. Four logins, four bills, and none of them have ever spoken to each other.

Four tools, nothing shared

Your theme doesn't know what your writer just published. Your automation rule doesn't know which drafts need a human first. Every join between them is a Zapier job that breaks the week somebody changes a plan.

A theme that argues back

Moving a section means five nested settings panels or a support ticket. By the time it's live you've lost the thread of whatever you were trying to say.

Published, then invisible

No llms.txt, no structured data, nothing telling an AI assistant the article exists. And nobody can flag a mistake on a live page without a screenshot and an email.

the tools aren't the problem — the gaps between them are. That's the bit Brian closed.

Content generator

Drafts that turn up before you ask for them.

Give it a topic and a few notes and it writes. Free, no limits, forever. Every draft goes through review first: approve it, send it back with notes, or bin it. Nothing reaches the live site on its own.

  • On-demand drafting from a topic and a few notes
  • Four editable presets — article, news, review, opinion — or write your own
  • Revision with feedback, not a blind regenerate button
  • Per-model usage and cost printed on every draft

Automation

Pro

Point it at every source that matters. It doesn't miss one.

Add as many RSS feeds as you like, from anything that publishes one — competitor blogs, trade press, your own sites, niche forums. It watches every source in parallel, dedupes by GUID so one story never becomes two drafts, and writes the moment something lands, routed to the topic, category and prompt you set for that feed.

  • Unlimited feeds, from anything that publishes RSS
  • A rule per source, with its own topic, tone, category and prompt
  • GUID deduping — one story in, one draft out, even across overlapping feeds
  • Runs on WordPress's own job queue. No external automation service, no extra bill

AI visibility

Readable and citable the moment you publish.

An assistant can't quote what it can't parse. ContentFlow keeps a live llms.txt, adds schema.org structured data, and serves a markdown REST API — so the crawlers that read you credit you properly instead of guessing.

  • Live llms.txt, kept in step with what you've published
  • Schema.org structured data, plus control over which AI crawlers get in
  • A markdown REST API built for language models, not just RSS readers

Feedback

A bug tracker for the things your articles get wrong.

Treat an out-of-date stat or a claim that no longer holds the way you'd treat a bug: highlight it on the live page and it's logged. On Pro, a fix for that exact passage is written for you and shown as a before-and-after. Every fix waits for a human to approve, edit or reject it — with tokenised links, so a client or an outside editor can review without an account.

  • Highlight-and-comment annotations straight onto live articles
  • A written fix for every flagged issue, as a before/after diffPro
  • Every fix held for a human. Nothing publishes itself
  • Resolve and triage with a full audit trail, not an open-ended thread
  • Tokenised review links — no account for outside reviewers

Fair questions

The three people ask first.

“Is this actually AI, or just a plugin?”

Both. AI is how it gets built and how it drafts. What lands on your site is ordinary, reliable WordPress that you own.

“What if it breaks?”

Brian built it, Brian supports it. One address to write to, not a ticket queue.

“What if we outgrow it?”

It's GPL and it's yours. Extend it, fork it, or hand it to a developer. There's no subscription to escape.
